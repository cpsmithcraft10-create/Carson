What we do

Three trades that all come down to
where the water goes.

Most properties need two of these and nobody ever told them. A lawn that will not hold green in August is usually a coverage problem, not a watering problem. Water in a basement is almost always a grade problem six feet outside it. We look at the whole property before we quote any part of it.

Sprinkler systems

Installed, repaired, extended, and put to bed for the winter.

We measure your static pressure and flow at the spigot before anything is drawn, because that number decides how many heads can run on a zone and nothing else does. Zones are laid out head to head — every head throwing far enough to reach the next one — so there is no dry crescent between them at the end of July. Rotors for open turf, sprays for the tight beds, drip where a spray would only wet the mulch and the siding.

We also take over other people’s systems. Valve that will not close, a zone that lost pressure, a controller nobody can programme, heads buried by four seasons of topdressing — that is a normal week.

  • Pressure tested at the source, not assumed
  • Zones matched precipitation within a zone
  • Backflow installed and tested to code
  • Winter blown out before the first hard freeze

Landscape lighting

The one thing you will notice every single night.

All low voltage, all warm — 2700 Kelvin, the colour of a lamp in a window, never the blue-white of a parking lot. Uplights at the base of the good trees, a narrow beam on the chimney or a stone face, soft wash across the front of the house, and only as much path lighting as it takes to see the step. The fixtures are solid brass or copper, because aluminium in a wet bed is a five-year fixture.

Before you spend anything we come back at dusk and set the fixtures out on the lawn on temporary leads, so you stand in your own driveway and look at the actual light on your actual house. Things get moved. They always do.

  • Colour 2700K, consistent fixture to fixture
  • Beams 12° to 60°, chosen per tree
  • Control astronomic timer, on at dusk year round
  • Metal brass and copper, buried splices sealed

Drainage

Water leaves the property. That is the whole job.

A wet basement, a lawn that stays spongy a week after rain, mulch washed across the driveway, a window well that fills — these are the same problem wearing different clothes. We find where the water is coming from and where the ground wants to send it, and then we give it a better way out: a French drain in gravel with a sock on the pipe, catch basins at the low points, downspouts tied in underground instead of dumping at the wall, and the grade fixed so the first six feet fall away from the house.

Everything runs to daylight where the fall allows it, and to a dry well where it does not. No pipe of ours ends in a guess.

  • Fall 1% minimum, shot with a laser level
  • Pipe 4″ perforated in a filter sock
  • Stone washed 3⁄4″, wrapped in fabric
  • Grade 6″ of fall in the first 10′

Questions

The ones we get asked every week.

Sprinklers

What does a sprinkler system actually cost?

It comes down to zones, and zones come down to your water. We measure the flow and pressure at the spigot; that tells us how many heads can run at once, which sets how many zones the property needs, which is most of the price. A small flat lot is a very different number from an acre on a slope with a well.

That is why the estimate is free and in writing. Anybody who quotes you a figure over the phone without asking about your water is guessing, and you will meet the difference later as a change order.

Are you going to tear up my lawn?

Far less than people expect. Pipe is pulled in with a vibratory plough, which slices the turf rather than trenching it out, so what you are left with is a seam you can walk on the same day. It knits back over a few weeks. Trenching only happens where we have to go under a drive or around roots we will not risk.

Do I need a backflow preventer, and does it need testing?

If your irrigation is fed off drinking water, yes — the device keeps lawn water from being drawn back into the house supply, and almost every town requires one. Many also require it to be tested annually by a certified tester and the result filed. We install it to code and handle the test.

When does it need winterising?

Before the first hard freeze — we start calling round in October. Compressed air is run through each zone until the lines are clear, because water left in a lateral splits the pipe and cracks the heads, and you find out in April. Spring start-up is the reverse: pressure back up slowly, every head checked and adjusted.

Lighting

Can I see it before I commit to it?

Yes, and we would rather you did. We come back at dusk with fixtures on temporary leads and set them out on the lawn, and you stand in your own driveway and look at your own house lit up. Beams get swapped, fixtures get moved back a foot. It costs you nothing and it is the difference between lighting you like and lighting you paid for.

How long do the fixtures last, and what does it cost to run?

LED lamps run tens of thousands of hours, so most people never touch them. The fixture itself is what fails, which is why we use solid brass and copper — they weather to a finish instead of corroding through like cast aluminium does in a wet bed.

Running cost is small. A typical front-of-house job is fifteen to twenty-five fixtures at two to four watts each — less than one old bulb in your porch light, running from dusk to dawn.

Drainage

Water gets into my basement. Can you fix it from outside?

Usually, and outside is where you should start. Most wet basements are not a foundation problem; they are roof water and grade. Downspouts dumping a few feet from the wall put hundreds of gallons against it in one storm, and ground that has settled back towards the house walks it straight down.

We tie the downspouts in underground, cut the grade so the first ten feet fall away, and add a French drain where the soil holds water. That fixes most of them. If it does not, you need somebody inside, and we will tell you that rather than sell you a trench.

Where does the water actually end up?

To daylight wherever the property has the fall for it — the pipe surfaces somewhere lower and open, and gravity does all of it, with nothing to maintain. Where there is no fall, it goes to a dry well sized for the roof area feeding it. What we will not do is run it to your property line and make it the neighbour’s problem.

Working with us

How soon can you come out?

A few days for a walk-through most of the year. March through May is the busiest stretch — everybody wants their system back on the same week — so a spring job is worth booking early. Broken mains and a yard filling with water get seen the same day.

Do you work on systems somebody else put in?

All the time. Repairs, taking over service, adding zones to a system that was undersized, replacing a controller with something you can actually use from your phone. We do not need to have installed it to look after it.

What is covered if something goes wrong?

Two years on parts and labour for anything we install, on top of the manufacturer’s cover on fixtures and controllers. If a head we set is throwing wrong next season, that is ours to put right.
